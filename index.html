Being a young single adult in Arkansas can be lonely

Utah has a YSA ward on every other corner. Central Arkansas has long drives and small congregations.

Spread across 18 congregations

Young adults in the two stakes are scattered over a couple hundred miles of highway, and many wards and branches have only a handful of them.

Lonelier than anyone lets on

61 percent of Americans ages 18 to 25 reported serious loneliness in a national survey. Sitting in a family ward as one of the only single adults can sharpen that on a Sunday.

Harvard Graduate School of Education, Making Caring Common, "Loneliness in America," 2021

Nobody should work out their faith alone

Questions about the scriptures, dating, school, work and doubt deserve a room full of people asking the same ones.

A weekly class made for the years between 18 and 35

Institute began in 1926 in Moscow, Idaho, for college students who wanted their faith to keep pace with their schooling. Today the Church's Seminaries and Institutes of Religion holds classes in nearly 2,700 locations, from campus buildings to living rooms.

Classes lean on discussion, and teachers are local members called by the stake. The Little Rock Stake class meets on Tuesday nights in a home in Benton.

Join a class in three steps

Members and friends of other faiths sign up the same way, and you can visit a class before you register.

  1. Pick your stake

    Open the stake tabs and find the class nearest where you live, work or study.

  2. Sign up on myInstitute

    Go to myinstitute.churchofjesuschrist.org and search for Little Rock. Members sign in with a Church account. Friends of other faiths can use Quick Signup with a name, an email and a birthdate.

  3. Come Tuesday night

    Bring scriptures or the Gospel Library app to 2710 Millbrook Drive, Benton on Tuesdays at 7:00 PM.

Earning institute credit

Credit counts toward institute graduation and, at many Church schools, toward religion requirements. To earn it:

  • Attend at least 75 percent of class meetings.
  • Finish the assigned reading: all of it for scripture courses, 75 percent for other courses.
  • Complete the course's learning experiences.

BYU, BYU-Idaho, BYU-Hawaii and Ensign College each set their own transfer rules.

Tuesday nights with people who share your faith

A year of institute in central Arkansas adds up to:

  • Friends from Little Rock to Hot Springs who know your name
  • A scripture habit that outlasts the semester
  • Room to ask the questions you have been carrying around

Without a class, too many young adults drift through their twenties alone, one quiet Sunday at a time.

Questions young adults ask first

Who can attend institute?

Anyone ages 18 to 35. Young single adults are the heart of the class, and married young adults are welcome. Where enough students sign up, classes split into ages 18 to 25 and 26 to 35. Adults over 35 can ask their ward or stake about other classes.

Is institute free?

Yes. Institute courses have no cost.

Do I have to be a member of the Church to attend?

No. Friends of other faiths are welcome. The myInstitute Quick Signup was built for them: it asks for a name, an email and a birthdate, with no Church account required, and the institute administrator approves the request.

Can I come without registering?

Yes. Visit a class first and register afterward. Registering near the start of a semester gives you the best chance of earning credit.
